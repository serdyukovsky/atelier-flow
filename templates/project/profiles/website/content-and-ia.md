# Content and information architecture

Use before implementing a new page or a major redesign.

## Define the page job

- What should a visitor understand in five seconds?
- What decision or action should happen next?
- What is the visitor's likely intent and prior knowledge?
- What objection or uncertainty must the page resolve?

## Plan the hierarchy

For each section, record:

- purpose;
- one message;
- supporting evidence;
- visual or asset needed;
- primary action, if any;
- mobile behavior.

Do not add a section only to fill space. Remove repeated explanations and duplicate CTA intent.

## Copy rules

- Prefer concrete product language over poetic filler.
- Do not invent facts, metrics, customers, reviews, guarantees, or technical claims.
- Keep the hero focused: one clear promise, one primary action, and only the necessary supporting context.
- Write functional labels for navigation, forms, errors, and actions.
- Treat copy as part of layout: check wrapping at desktop and mobile widths.

## Deliverable

Before implementation, produce a short page outline and identify missing content. If missing content materially changes the design, ask the user; otherwise mark a temporary placeholder explicitly.
